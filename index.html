[ 02 · What the research showed ]

The current site makes the buyer work too hard before they even contact AGOL.

This is more than a redesign. Search, clarity, trust and conversion all work together.

01 — SEARCH / SECURITY

The current site is not a strong base for growth.

The audit found casino spam on 11 of 13 reviewed pages, plus indexing and technical issues. These problems must be fixed before we build a stronger SEO strategy.

02 — CLARITY

The buyer has to work too hard to understand AGOL.

Time-Critical, Global Trade and Supply Chain are strong services, but the current site does not explain them in a simple order.

03 — CONVERSION

Ready-to-buy visitors can hit dead ends.

Some quote, phone and email links do not work correctly. The new site should send each buyer to the right person or quote form.

A buyer under pressure should not have to investigate AGOL to understand AGOL.

[ 07 · Trust system ]

Trust should be visible, specific and real.

For a serious logistics buyer, saying “we can handle it” is not enough. The website needs real proof.

Capabilities

Verified service proof.

Only publish claims AGOL can prove: coverage, facilities, service availability and real capabilities.

Credentials

Certifications with context.

Confirm IATA, CTPAT, TSA IAC and any other certifications before launch, then show them where they help build trust.

Stories

Real operations, not generic promises.

Case studies, reviews and real shipment stories can answer a simple question: “Can AGOL handle my situation?”

[ 08A · Buyer journey ]

Not every buyer finds AGOL through Google. But the website still helps them decide if they trust AGOL.

The brief shows two main paths: discovery and validation. The new website has to work for both.

They search for a solution.

02Relevant service, industry or country page
03Proof that AGOL can handle the situation

Now they need confidence.

01Referral, relationship, outreach or LinkedIn

[ 08B · Buyer questions → website answers ]

The website should answer the questions buyers already have.

These questions come directly from what AGOL hears in real sales conversations.

“Are you big enough to handle this?”
Show real proof. Use credentials, facilities, real operations, case studies and approved customer proof instead of general claims.
“Do you really have coverage where I need it?”
Show where AGOL can really operate. Use clear coverage information and country details where they are verified.
“Can you handle the compliance side there?”
Explain IOR / EOR in simple steps. Show what AGOL handles, what the client provides and how the process works.
“Why not just use a large integrator?”
Show the specialist advantage. One team that connects compliance and urgent logistics is more specific than saying only “global logistics.”
“Will someone actually answer when it matters?”
Make human contact easy. Urgent buyers should reach the right team fast. Any 24/7 wording should use AGOL’s approved wording.
